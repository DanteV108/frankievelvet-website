The Society

The Tendertones Appreciation Society exists because Marjorie Croll thought somebody ought to be writing this down, and because nobody in the band was going to.

Origins

Nine members and a borrowed Gestetner.

Marjorie Croll named the band at twenty to two in the morning on 15 February 1991, at her daughter’s wedding, and spent the following six years attending its engagements without comment. In March 1997, having noticed that the Chandelier Room residency had passed four hundred performances and that nobody had recorded any of them, she founded the Society with nine members, a card index and a duplicating machine borrowed from the Preston Bowls Club.

She was president for twenty-two years. She died in 2019, three days after correcting the proofs of Bulletin no. 88. Colleen Whitrod, member no. 14, has been president since.

The Society keeps the photographic archive, the engagement diaries, the tape collection, the Charter itself, and 1,206 logged requests. It has published The Tender Bulletin every quarter for twenty-nine years without missing an issue, including the quarter in which its president died and the quarter in which its printer closed.

“They will not keep a single thing. They will not keep a poster, they will not keep a review, one of them threw out the master tapes. Somebody has to be sentimental on their behalf and it may as well be me.” Marjorie Croll · Bulletin no. 1, June 1997

How to join

By post, on a card, with your name, your address and the year you first saw the band. The Society does not have a website form and has voted twice against having one.

The second vote, in 2021, was lost by eleven votes to one hundred and four. The one was Lionel Bright.

The Tender Bulletin

One hundred and sixteen issues. Not one missed.

Twelve pages, quarterly, duplicated until 2004 and photocopied since. Contents are always in the same order: engagements past, engagements coming, a plate from the archive, a member remembered, corrections, and the Requests Log.

From Bulletin no. 116

June 2026 · Ed. C. Whitrod · Twelve pages, stapled

ENGAGEMENTS PAST. Twenty-three since the last issue, of which nineteen were the Friday residency. The band played to a full room on all but two Fridays; on 8 May there were forty-one people, which Mr Tiplady attributed to the football.

CORRECTIONS. In no. 115 we stated that Nino Pesic’s first engagement was 14 February 1991. It was. We also stated that he was 46 at the time. He was 45 and turned 46 in the April. Our thanks to Mr K. Lambourne (member no. 203) who has now corrected us on this point in three separate decades.

A MEMBER REMEMBERED. Doreen Vasey, member no. 7, of Fawkner, died in April aged 94. Doreen attended 611 Chandelier Room performances — every one — and 1,900 engagements in total, more than any person living or dead including every member of the band. She always sat at table nine. The song is named for her and she never once mentioned it.

REQUESTS LOG. Eleven requests taken this quarter. Nine were known to the band. Two were not and were played regardless, in accordance with Article III. One of the two has since been identified by Mr Reis as a Portuguese hymn.

Back issues 1–116 are held in the Society’s room and may be consulted by appointment. Issues 1–40 exist as single copies only and may not be photocopied, following an incident in 2006.

Bookings

Weddings, functions, wakes and anything with chairs.

The band has no manager and no agent. Enquiries go to the Society, which passes them to Bettina Croll, who passes them to Frankie on a Thursday. An answer usually takes eleven days and has never once been no.

An Appeal

If you were there, the Society would like to hear from you.

Photographs, negatives, tickets, programmes, posters, function running sheets, cassettes, wedding videos and drinks lists. Items are copied and returned within a fortnight. Six hundred and four plates in the collection are undated and the Society would dearly like that number to go down.

Particularly sought: any photograph of the interior of the Chandelier Room, of which the Society holds two and neither negative survives; the 1998 New Zealand tour, of which it holds nothing at all; and the power supply for a Seagate external hard drive, model ST3320, containing the final Neptune Lounge season.
