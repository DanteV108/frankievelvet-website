Established 14 February 1991, Thornbury

Thirty-five years of
tenderness, nightly.

They have never had a hit. They have never had a night off, either.

Frankievelvet and the Tendertones have been performing continuously since a Valentine’s Day wedding in 1991 at which they were not, strictly speaking, the booked act. In the thirty-five years since, thirty-one musicians have passed through the band, four of them twice. They have played reception centres, bowls clubs, a demolished hotel in St Kilda, six seasons of the Pacific cruise circuit, a casino terrace in Portugal they reached by administrative error, and ninety-one consecutive nights from a balcony in Reservoir.

The repertoire is what it has always been: the standards, sung slightly wrong, at length, with total conviction. This site is the collected archive of that work — every recording, every documented engagement, every review that has survived, and the papers of the Society that has kept them.

The Charter

Written on the reverse of a Hotel Sirocco drinks list, 2 June 1994. Framed above the bar until the building came down.

  1. Tenderness, nightly.
  2. No stick shall touch a drum head.
  3. Every request is honoured, whether or not it is known.
  4. A departing Tendertone nominates his own replacement.
  5. The band does not stop for applause, and does not stop for its absence.
  6. Nobody discusses the arrangement in front of the audience.
  7. The last song is always slow.

Article II was added in 1998 at the insistence of Warren Tiplady, who has not used a drumstick in public since. Article IV is the reason the band still exists.

The Act

What, precisely, is the Velvet Sound?

The band has never accepted the label “lounge.” Frankie Velvet describes the repertoire as “continental” and the delivery as “unhurried.” What this means in practice is a Hammond organ set to its warmest drawbar, brushes on a snare, a tenor saxophone entering roughly a bar and a half after everyone expects it, and a frontman who sings the standards at approximately three-quarter speed as though delivering news to a relative.

In April 1996 the critic Neville Marsh of The Melbourne Weekly Observer reviewed the Chandelier Room residency and coined the phrase that has followed the band ever since:

“It is not lounge and it is not jazz. It is Preston Baroque — music of enormous ornament and no urgency whatsoever, performed by men in ruffled shirts for an audience that has already eaten.” Neville Marsh · The Melbourne Weekly Observer · 12 April 1996

Marsh intended this unkindly. Within eighteen months it was printed on the band’s posters, and by 1999 on the bass drum head. When Nocturne Reissues of Berlin licensed the Vellum catalogue in 2014, they titled the box set Preston Baroque without asking, and nobody objected.

“A guitarist joins this band and inherits a guitar. It is not a gift. It is a responsibility.” — F.V., 2013

The Signature Number

“Do You Know the Way to Santa Fe?”

Frankie Velvet has performed the Bacharach–David standard under this title since 14 February 1991. He has been corrected, in writing, by at least nine people, including two members of his own band, a cruise director, a Portuguese promoter and, in 2023, a publishing body. He has never altered a syllable.

Asked about it by Australian Cabaret Review in 1997, he said only: “I’ve been there. It’s lovely.” Asked again in 2024, he said: “We’ve covered this.”

The matter was formally settled in November 2023 with a licensed “reinterpretation” credit. He still sings Santa Fe.

The Long Ipanema

The band’s arrangement of “The Girl from Ipanema” has grown by roughly ninety seconds per year since 1994. It has never been shortened. Nobody has ever proposed shortening it.

The Long Goodnight

Recorded across four Sunday afternoons at the Golden Lotus Reception Centre, Thornbury — the room in which the band was formed, now under different management and better lighting. Eleven songs, nine of them slow. Del Auguste’s Hammond is the loudest thing on the record; Frankie is mixed, at his own request, “a little further back than usual, as a courtesy.”

The Tendertones Appreciation Society

Four hundred and twelve members. Nine of them founding.

The Society was founded in 1997 by Marjorie Croll — the woman who named the band at 1.40am in 1991 — and has published The Tender Bulletin quarterly, without interruption, ever since. Issue 117 is at the printer.

Membership includes the Bulletin, advance notice of engagements, and the right to request one song per calendar year, which under Article III must be honoured.
