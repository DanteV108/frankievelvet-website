1991 — 2026

The Legend

A complete history of the act, assembled from the band’s own diaries, the Society’s minute books, four surviving posters and one very persistent bootleg.

I. The Founding

Saint Valentine’s Day, 1991

The Astoria Reception Centre on High Street, Thornbury, had booked the Norm Castellan Trio for the Whitrod–Croll wedding on the evening of 14 February 1991. The trio was, at that hour, in Wodonga, at a different wedding, having accepted two engagements on the same day in the belief that one of them was a Saturday.

Also booked, for forty minutes across the meal at a fee of sixty dollars, was a thirty-year-old photocopier salesman from Preston named Francis Velletri, billed on the running sheet as “Frankie Velvet — interval songs.”

At 8.15pm the function manager informed Mr Velletri that there was no band. At 8.40pm he returned to the stage with three men he had found in the room:

  • Barry Sloane, the venue’s own organist, who had been on his way home;
  • Ken Pantelis, the bride’s uncle, who had played drums in the army;
  • Ivan “Nino” Pesic, a refrigeration mechanic servicing the air-conditioning, who kept a tenor saxophone in his van for reasons he never satisfactorily explained.

They played for four hours and ten minutes. They performed “Do You Know the Way to Santa Fe?” eleven times, because it was the only chart Barry Sloane could read in the key Frankie sang it in. Nobody left.

At approximately 1.40am the bride’s mother, Marjorie Croll, was heard to say to the function manager: “Well, they’re a lot of tender tones, aren’t they.” Barry Sloane wrote it on the back of a place card. The place card is in the Society’s collection and is the band’s founding document.

“I went in as the bloke who sings between the courses. I came out as a band. I have never been entirely sure that I agreed to it.” Frankie Velvet, 2024
A note on the spelling

The act is billed as one word. This originates with a printer in Brunswick who in March 1993 set the Golden Fleece poster as FRANKIEVELVET AND THE TENDERTONES and delivered two hundred of them before anyone saw a proof.

Frankie declined to have them reprinted, on the grounds that the money had been spent. The spelling has been used on every poster, sleeve and cheque butt since. He himself remains Frankie Velvet, two words, in conversation and on his licence.

II. The Record

Thirty-five years, in order.

Dates are taken from the band’s engagement diaries, which have survived complete except for 2003, which Frankie used to prop up a caravan.

  1. 1991

    The Astoria

    Formed 14 February. In August the band takes its first residency: Tuesday nights at the Golden Fleece Hotel, Brunswick, for the door and a counter meal. It runs nineteen months. Repertoire at this point is forty-one songs, of which eleven are learned wrong and stay that way.

  2. 1992

    Article IV is born

    Ken Pantelis leaves in June with a back injury sustained at a different wedding. Rather than audition, he simply arrives one night with a replacement — Dougie Farr — and introduces him from the stage. The practice becomes law two years later as Article IV of the Charter, and is the single reason the band has never once disbanded for lack of members.

  3. 1993

    Velvet by Candlelight

    Five hundred cassettes dubbed two at a time in Joan Velletri’s garage in Preston over eleven consecutive Sundays. Sleeve photocopied at Nu-Tone Business Machines, where Frankie was still employed, on paper he later described as “surplus.” The Brunswick printer sets the name as one word. It stays that way.

  4. 1994

    The Chandelier Room

    Ron Vellum of Vellum Records — a label operating from the storeroom of the Northcote Milk Bar — signs the band on a single page of foolscap. In April they begin a Wednesday-to-Saturday residency in the Chandelier Room of the Hotel Sirocco, St Kilda. It runs six years and 611 performances. On 2 June, on the reverse of a drinks list, the Tendertone Charter is written.

  5. 1995

    The first album, and the first chart placing

    Do You Know the Way to Santa Fe? reaches No. 4 on the Victorian Independent Cassette Chart, a chart which existed for eleven weeks and was compiled by a man in Reservoir. Rhonda Pike and Marj Delahunty join as backing vocalists and are billed, from the first night, only as the Velvettes.

  6. 1996

    Preston Baroque — and the Tuxedo Schism

    On 12 April, Neville Marsh reviews the residency for The Melbourne Weekly Observer and coins the phrase that defines the band. Six weeks later, on 30 May, bassist Sonny Kane and drummer Dougie Farr walk out mid-interval over a proposal to introduce ruffled shirts. They form Sonny Kane and the Velveteens. Neither party has spoken to the other on the record since.

  7. 1997

    Ipanema After Midnight; the Society is founded

    Warren “Waz” Tiplady joins on drums. Marjorie Croll founds the Tendertones Appreciation Society with nine members and produces the first Tender Bulletin on a Gestetner borrowed from a bowls club. The band formally adopts “Preston Baroque” on its posters.

  8. 1998

    The Trans-Tasman Tenderness Tour

    Nine dates in New Zealand, of which seven occur. Tiplady, having broken a stick and a snare head on the same night in Palmerston North, switches permanently to brushes; Article II is added to the Charter on the return flight.

  9. 1999

    The Y2K Midnight Set

    On 3 October, at the Sirocco, “The Girl from Ipanema” runs 41 minutes and 12 seconds — still the record. On 31 December, at 11.58pm, the power fails. The band completes the set acoustically by cigarette lighter and finishes at 1.20am to a room that had been told to go home. A bootleg cassette of the last forty minutes circulates for eleven years before the Society releases it officially.

  10. 2000–2001

    The end of the Sirocco

    The hotel is sold in March 2000. Last Chandelier Room performance: 30 June. On 14 March 2001 the building is demolished. The band does not announce a break-up and does not play again for three and a half years. Frankie takes a position selling above-ground swimming pools.

  11. 2002

    Songs for an Empty Function Room

    Recorded alone, on a four-track, in a caravan at Rosebud, across nine days in August. One hundred cassettes. Nineteen sold. Frankie gives most of the rest away at the pool yard. It is now generally held to be the best thing the act has ever done, a judgement he regards as “a bit rich.”

  12. 2004

    The wake

    Norm Castellan — the bandleader who never arrived in 1991, and afterwards a friend to all of them — dies on 12 September. At the wake, in a function room in Fairfield, Sloane sits down at the piano, Nino gets his horn out of the car, Tiplady plays a chair with two beer coasters, and Frankie sings “Santa Fe.” The band reforms that afternoon and has not stopped since.

  13. 2005–2011

    The Cruise Years

    Six seasons aboard the M.S. Coral Duchess on the Pacific circuit: two sets a night, six nights a week, plus the Captain’s Farewell. Over eight hundred performances at sea. Frankie later described the era as “the most work we ever did and the least anyone noticed.”

  14. 2008

    The Estoril Misunderstanding

    A promoter in Cascais, believing he had engaged a Dutch DJ named Franky Velvet, books the band for a Saturday on the casino terrace. Seven hundred people arrive expecting house music. The band plays anyway, in white dinner jackets, in thirty-one degrees. Nine encores. They have returned to Portugal every year since.

  15. 2011–2013

    Marco, Del, and the rediscovery

    Marco Reis, who has followed the band around the Lisbon coast for four years, joins on bass in 2011. In 2012 Hartmut Bell of Nocturne Reissues, Berlin, licenses Songs for an Empty Function Room for vinyl on the strength of a cassette bought in a Melbourne op shop; it sells four thousand copies in Europe. Delphine Auguste, a church organist from Footscray, joins in 2013. The three Velvetmania nights at the Palais Rialto sell out in an afternoon.

  16. 2014

    Preston Baroque: The Vellum Years

    Nocturne’s three-LP box collects everything Vellum released between 1993 and 1999, with a forty-page booklet the band was not consulted on. Sim Lachlan reviews it at length in Grooveyard Monthly and flies to Melbourne to see them, twice.

  17. 2016

    Nino

    Ivan “Nino” Pesic dies on 8 November, aged 71, after twenty-five years and one month in the tenor chair — the longest continuous service of any Tendertone but Frankie himself. The chair is left empty for a full year. A stand and an unplayed microphone are set for him at every engagement in 2017.

  18. 2017–2019

    For Nino, and a farewell that wasn’t

    Bettina Croll — Marjorie’s granddaughter, and the band’s accountant since 2009 — takes the tenor chair under Article IV, having been nominated by Nino himself in a letter dated eight months before his death. In 2018 the band undertakes the Farewell Forever Tour, eleven dates, and returns fourteen months later. Marjorie Croll dies in 2019; Colleen Whitrod succeeds her as president of the Society.

  19. 2020

    The Balcony Sessions

    From 26 March, Frankie sings from the balcony of his flat in Reservoir at 7pm each evening, accompanied by a portable amplifier and, from the seventh night, Del Auguste on a keyboard set up in the car park below. Ninety-one consecutive nights. The recordings are made on a neighbour’s phone and released, unedited, as Live from the Balcony (Reservoir).

  20. 2021–2022

    Lionel, and the Long Ipanema on record

    Lionel Bright, nineteen, joins on guitar after a clip of him playing “Nino’s Waltz” reaches the band by three separate routes in one week. In 2022 the band finally commits the Long Ipanema to record — 38 minutes 51 seconds, one track, two sides — and Priya Ramanathan’s documentary Still Tender premieres at Northcote Independent Film Week to a sold-out house of two hundred and six.

  21. 2023

    The Santa Fe Affair

    Thirty-two years after the fact, a publishing body writes to the band about the title of its signature number. The full correspondence is set out below. Settled in November with a licensed “reinterpretation” credit. He still sings Santa Fe.

  22. 2024–2025

    Back to the room

    For the 33rd anniversary the band takes a six-week residency at the Golden Lotus Reception Centre, Thornbury — the former Astoria, the room in which it was formed. The live album from those nights is the band’s best-selling record. In 2025 The Velvet Songbook pairs eleven of the originals with the Northern Suburbs Chamber Ensemble, at Del Auguste’s insistence and Frankie’s visible discomfort.

  23. 2026

    The Long Goodnight

    The fourteenth studio album, and a Friday residency at the Emerald Room, Coburg, of no announced duration. Frankie is sixty-five. Asked in August whether the title meant anything, he said: “It means the last song is always slow. It’s in the Charter. You’ve got a copy.”

III. The Santa Fe Affair

“I’ve been there. It’s lovely.”

From the first night at the Astoria, Frankie Velvet has performed the Bacharach–David standard as “Do You Know the Way to Santa Fe?” No document survives explaining why. Barry Sloane maintained until his death that Frankie sang it correctly at the top of the evening and incorrectly by the fourth pass, and that by the eleventh “it had set.”

He has been corrected in writing by at least nine people. Two were members of his own band. One was a cruise director. One, in 2008, was a Portuguese promoter with a laminated printout. In every case the correction was received courteously and no change was made.

“Nobody has ever come up to me after a show and said they couldn’t find San Jose. Whereas I have had four people tell me they went to Santa Fe because of us. Two of them sent postcards. That is the whole of my defence.” Frankie Velvet · Tone Arm, January 2024

IV. The Tuxedo Schism

Thirty years of not speaking, over a shirt.

On 30 May 1996, between the second and third sets at the Hotel Sirocco, Frankie Velvet proposed that the rhythm section adopt ruffled dress shirts, in oxblood, to match the front line. Bassist Sonny Kane replied that he would not, and that he had never agreed to any of it. Drummer Dougie Farr left with him through the kitchen. Neither returned for the third set, which the remaining four played as a quartet and which several people present described as the best of the residency.

Within eleven weeks Kane had formed Sonny Kane and the Velveteens, an act with a repertoire substantially identical to the Tendertones’ and a strict no-ruffle policy. They are still working. Their poster reads “The Original Sound — Plain Shirts Only.”

Kane has claimed authorship of “Moonlight Over Merri Creek” in three separate interviews. The song is registered to Velletri, F. X., and was performed at the Golden Fleece eight months before Kane joined the band.

“Sonny’s a good bass player and a bad witness.” Frankie Velvet, 2014 — his only public comment in thirty years

On 8 August 2015 both acts were booked to the Preston Bowls Club on the same evening. They played alternate sets until 11.40pm without acknowledging one another. Takings were divided evenly. The Society records the night as “cordial.”

V. Estoril, 2008

Seven hundred people, expecting a Dutchman.

The booking arrived by email in February 2008 and was accepted by Frankie on the basis that it was in Portugal and the fee was in euros. The promoter, João Bettencourt of Cascais, had been trying for some weeks to engage a Rotterdam DJ trading as Franky Velvet, and had found what he believed to be his management.

The error became apparent at 9.40pm on Saturday 12 July, when six men and three women in white dinner jackets carried a Hammond organ onto the terrace of the Casino Estoril in front of a crowd that had been promised house music until four in the morning.

Bettencourt’s account, given to Ruído the following month, is the only first-hand record:

“I understood at once what I had done. I went to the sound man to stop it. And then the old one with the saxophone played eight notes and I sat down on the step and I did not get up again until two o’clock.” João Bettencourt · Ruído no. 31, August 2008

The band played three hours and twenty minutes and took nine encores. “The Girl from Ipanema” ran twenty-two minutes and eighteen seconds; a section of the crowd sang the horn line back. They have returned to the Lisbon coast every year since, and in 2011 acquired a Portuguese bass player who had been at the front on the night.

Frankie has never learned more than eleven words of Portuguese and uses all of them, in order, at every Portuguese engagement, as an introduction. Marco Reis has described the speech as “technically incorrect and completely irreplaceable.”

VI. The Founding Document

The Tendertone Charter

Written by Barry Sloane at Frankie’s dictation on the reverse of a Hotel Sirocco drinks list, 2 June 1994, after an argument about whether to acknowledge a heckler. Seven articles. Framed above the Chandelier Room bar until March 2001, when it was removed by Nino Pesic the night before the demolition and kept in his van for fifteen years.

It now hangs in the Society’s room in Preston. It has never been amended, only added to: Articles II and VII are in a different hand.

  1. Tenderness, nightly.
  2. No stick shall touch a drum head.
  3. Every request is honoured, whether or not it is known.
  4. A departing Tendertone nominates his own replacement.
  5. The band does not stop for applause, and does not stop for its absence.
  6. Nobody discusses the arrangement in front of the audience.
  7. The last song is always slow.
Article III in practice

Requests are honoured whether or not the band knows the song. Where it does not, the Tendertones play what they believe the song to be. The Society has logged 1,206 requests since 1997, of which an estimated 340 were performed incorrectly and 11 were performed correctly by accident.
