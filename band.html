Thirty-one Musicians, One Chair at a Time

The Band

Under Article IV of the Charter, no Tendertone is auditioned. Each is nominated by the member he or she replaces. The line-up below is therefore an unbroken chain back to a wedding in Thornbury in 1991.

The Frontman

Frankie Velvet

Voice, maracas, emotional direction · Founder · 1991–

Born Francis Xavier Velletri in Preston on 3 August 1961, the son of Salvatore Velletri, a tiler from Reggio Calabria, and Joan Velletri (née Dabb), a piano teacher from Ballarat who gave him eleven years of lessons he describes as “wasted on the technique and worth it for the manners.”

He sold photocopiers for Nu-Tone Business Machines from 1983 to 1994 and above-ground swimming pools from 2001 to 2004, and has never in thirty-five years described singing as his occupation on a form. He performed as Frank Vallory in 1984 and as Frankie Vellini and the Continentals from 1988 to 1990, an act consisting entirely of himself.

He wears a white dinner jacket and an oxblood shirt, and has worn no tie since 1997. He drives a 1994 Ford Fairlane which the band calls the Office. He lives in Reservoir, four streets from where he was born, and has done since 1996.

“People say we’re a nostalgia act. We’re not. Nostalgia is when you miss something. We never stopped.” Frankie Velvet · Still Tender (2022)

The Current Line-Up

Six Tendertones and three Velvettes.

As at 1 September 2026. Combined service: one hundred and eleven years.

II

Warren “Waz” Tiplady

Drums · Joined 1997 · Nominated by Col Brindle

A refrigeration engineer by trade, like his predecessor in the band’s affections, and the longest-serving Tendertone now playing. Tiplady has not used a drumstick in public since 4 September 1998, when he broke a stick and a snare head in the same bar in Palmerston North and decided, on the flight home, that the whole business was too loud. Article II of the Charter was added at his insistence that week and has never been tested.

He owns eleven pairs of brushes and keeps them in a cutlery tray.

III

Marco Reis

Double bass, electric bass · Joined 2011 · Nominated by Kevin Hallward

Present at the Casino Estoril on 12 July 2008, in the third row, having come for the Dutch DJ. He followed the band around the Lisbon coast for three summers before anyone learned his name, and was nominated to the chair in 2011 by a retiring Kevin Hallward on the grounds that he was “there anyway.”

He lives in Cascais and flies to Melbourne for the season. He is the only Tendertone who has read the Charter in translation, and the only one who can quote it.

IV

Delphine “Del” Auguste

Hammond organ, backing voice, arrangements · Joined 2013 · Nominated by Ray Mifsud

Fifteen years a church organist in Footscray before a Tuesday night at the Palais Rialto changed the course of her Sundays. Auguste is the band’s only formally trained musician and its only arranger; she is responsible for The Velvet Songbook (2025), which she proposed, costed and delivered before informing Frankie it was happening.

She sets the Hammond to the same three drawbars every night and has refused, on the record, to say which.

V

Bettina Croll

Tenor saxophone · Accounts · Joined 2017 · Nominated by Ivan Pesic

Granddaughter of Marjorie Croll, who named the band in 1991 and founded the Society in 1997. Bettina kept the band’s books from 2009 and played tenor only at home. Nino Pesic nominated her to succeed him in a letter dated 3 March 2016, eight months before his death, which he gave to Del Auguste to hold and which nobody else saw until the following November.

She took the chair in November 2017, after it had stood empty for a year, and still does the accounts.

VI

Lionel Bright

Guitar · Joined 2021 · Nominated by Stefan Kolar

Nineteen when he joined and the youngest Tendertone by twenty-six years. A recording of Bright playing “Nino’s Waltz” alone in a garage in Sunshine reached the band by three unconnected routes in the same week in March 2021; Stefan Kolar, who had been looking for a reason to retire, nominated him without meeting him.

He plays the house archtop — the fifth Tendertone to do so — and is, by the Charter, its custodian rather than its owner.

VII

The Velvettes

Backing voices · Rhonda Pike (1995) · Marj Delahunty (1995) · Kaye Osborne (2006)

Never billed individually, at their own request, since their first engagement in October 1995. Pike and Delahunty met at a Preston bowls club and joined together on the condition that they would not be separated in the running order; Kaye Osborne joined at sea in 2006 and completed the trio.

They stand stage left, in that order, and have done for thirty-one years. Between them they have missed nine engagements, four of which were the same wedding.

1991–2016

The chair that stayed empty for a year.

Ivan “Nino” Pesic played tenor saxophone with this band for twenty-five years and one month, from the night he walked in to service an air-conditioning unit to a Thursday in Coburg eleven days before he died. He never missed an engagement. Not one, in twenty-five years — not for the cruise seasons, not for the Wilderness Years, when there were no engagements to miss and he turned up anyway to Frankie’s flat on Thursdays with the horn in the car.

After his death on 8 November 2016 the band played the whole of 2017 with a stand, a microphone and an empty chair set stage right, unplugged. Fifty-two engagements. Nobody mentioned it from the stage and nobody had to.

“He came to fix the cooling. He stayed twenty-five years. That is the whole story of this band and I have never been able to improve on it.” Frankie Velvet, at the Northcote Town Hall, 19 November 2016

Deputies & One-Night Tendertones

Under Article IV a deputy who completes a full engagement is a Tendertone. Five names are held on the Roll on this basis: Ted Mullane (organ, one night, 1998), Pat Whelan (drums, two nights, 2002), Simone Baïa (voice, one night, Estoril, 2008), Hartmut Bell (tambourine, one night, Berlin, 2014, badly), and Norm Castellan, who was never in the band and is listed first in every printed programme.

Standing Orders

Dress, and other matters of policy.

The band has no manager, no contract and no written agreement of any kind beyond the Charter. It does, however, have thirty-five years of settled practice, which the Society has compiled and which the band has declined to confirm or deny.

  • Jacketswhite, after 1994
  • Shirtsoxblood; ruffles optional since 1996
  • Tiesabandoned 1997
  • RehearsalThursdays, Frankie’s flat, since 1996
  • Set lengthfourteen songs, two sets
  • Encoresas many as are asked for
  • Paymentdivided equally, including the deputies
  • The last songalways slow (Article VII)

“The ruffles were never compulsory. That is the part nobody believes.” — F.V., 2018
