Melbourne, since 1991

Thirty-five years
of the same songs.

A nine-piece continental act working out of the northern suburbs.

Frankievelvet and the Tendertones have been performing without a break since February 1991, when they were put together in an afternoon at a reception centre in Thornbury. The line-up has changed thirty-one times. The repertoire has changed very little.

In that time the band has held residencies at the Golden Fleece in Brunswick, the Chandelier Room in St Kilda, and the Neptune Lounge of the M.S. Coral Duchess on the Pacific circuit. It plays weddings, functions and the occasional funeral. It has returned to the Lisbon coast every year since 2008. It is currently at the Emerald Room in Coburg on Fridays.

Fourteen studio records, six live. Most of them were sold at the side of the stage.

How the band works

Nobody auditions. When a Tendertone leaves, they nominate the person who takes the chair, and the band takes them. It has worked this way since 1992 and is the reason the act has never once stopped for want of a player.

Two sets of fourteen songs, twenty minutes between them. Requests are taken. Brushes, not sticks. The last song is always slow.

The guitar belongs to the band rather than to whoever is playing it. It was bought second-hand in Preston in 1993 and has had five custodians.

The Sound

Continental, unhurried.

Hammond organ on its warmest setting, brushes on a snare, tenor saxophone, upright bass, an archtop guitar and three voices behind the lead. The standards, taken slowly. The band has never used the word lounge about itself and has never much minded other people using it.

In April 1996 Neville Marsh reviewed the St Kilda residency for The Melbourne Weekly Observer and described the act in a phrase that has been on the posters ever since:

“It is not lounge and it is not jazz. It is Preston Baroque — music of considerable ornament and no urgency whatever, played for an audience that has already eaten.” Neville Marsh · The Melbourne Weekly Observer · 12 April 1996

He did not intend it as a compliment. He reviewed the band nine times.

Bookings

Weddings, functions and anything with chairs.

The band has no manager. Enquiries go to Bettina Croll, who also plays tenor saxophone and keeps the books, and who will usually come back to you within a fortnight.

Two sets, an interval, and whatever is asked for at the end. Six weeks’ notice is preferred. Less has been managed.
